Both directions, one integration. Collections and disbursements are the same problem in a construction workflow, and they are usually two vendors.

Money coming in

Progress payments, draws, and owner or lender funding, requested from inside your product with the job and the invoice already attached.

Change orders alter the amount without altering the plumbing, because the amount is a field rather than a new integration.

Money going out

Subcontractor and supplier payouts, released on the conditions you already enforce: inspection passed, lien waiver received, retainage schedule met.

Your platform decides when. Payload moves it and writes the result back against the project, the job and the invoice.

Questions a platform team asks first.

Who holds the funds?

This is the question that decides your licensing, your liability and your audit surface, and it is expensive to change later. It is the first thing to settle, before any timeline estimate means anything.

Do we have to become a payment facilitator?

No. Processing accounts are created and underwritten through Payload, with automated KYC and identity verification, so your platform onboards merchants without taking on facilitator status itself.

How long does an integration actually take?

The honest answer depends on how much of the flow you want to own. Onboarding, the money movement, reconciliation and the support view are four separate pieces, and built alone they are quarters rather than sprints. That is what the conversation is for.

Can we keep our own approval rules?

Yes, and you should. Your platform already knows whether the inspection passed and whether the lien waiver is on file. Payload moves money when you say so rather than asking you to rebuild those rules somewhere else.

What happens to reconciliation?

Results are written back against the project, the job and the invoice they belong to, which is the thing a separate bank portal cannot do because it never had the context.
